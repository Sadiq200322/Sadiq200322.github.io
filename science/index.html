The science behind Neupulse

Neupulse C is a wrist wearable that uses gentle, rhythmic stimulation to support a sense of calm. It builds on over 20 years of neuroscience research led by our founders at the University of Nottingham.

Our research began in 2005, exploring Tourette syndrome and the brain networks involved in movement and cognitive control. Early studies used brain imaging and stimulation to understand how changes in brain function relate to the natural variation of tics over time.

In 2009, work with the UK charity Tourettes Action, the NIHR and Nottingham Biomedical Research Centre highlighted the need for approaches that could be used more easily outside a clinic.

The team investigated non-invasive ways of interacting with brain activity, including methods indicated to influence neural rhythms associated with motor control. Techniques such as transcranial magnetic stimulation were explored, but they were not practical for everyday use.

That led to rhythmic median nerve stimulation as a more accessible alternative. Supported by the University of Nottingham, Tourettes Action, and the NIHR Nottingham Biomedical Research Centre, this work became the broader Neupulse platform.

Clinical studies, including a UK double-blind trial, have explored this approach in people with tic disorders. Findings indicated changes in tic-related measures under certain conditions. The research continues to inform clinical development.

The medical device, Neupulse M, is under development and moving through regulatory approval. Neupulse C is the wellness form of the same approach, made for general wellbeing.
